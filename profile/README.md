## What Is IPTV and Why Are Americans and Canadians Cutting the Cord?

IPTV stands for Internet Protocol Television. Instead of a cable box or satellite dish, your TV channels stream directly over your internet connection. One subscription delivers live TV, sports, and on-demand movies — on every device you already own, with no long-term contract.

The numbers tell the story. The average American household pays over $150 a month for a cable TV package stuffed with channels nobody watches. In Canada, the situation is identical. Cable subscriptions in the USA and Canada can cost $80–$150 per month.

A quality IPTV subscription costs $10–$20 per month — or as little as $5–$8 per month on an annual plan. Annual savings with IPTV versus Comcast Xfinity can reach up to $1,375 per year. Against Bell Fibe in Canada, the saving is up to $1,550 CAD per year.

The math is straightforward. More channels, better sports coverage, 4K quality — for a fraction of what cable charges.

## What to Look for in a USA & Canada IPTV Service

**US and Canadian channels included** — Any serious service must carry the major broadcast networks: ABC, CBS, NBC, Fox, PBS, CTV, CBC, Global, and City TV at a minimum.

**Sports coverage** — This is the primary reason most Americans and Canadians switch. Look for NFL, NBA, NHL, MLB, UFC/PPV, NASCAR, and college sports via ESPN, TNT, TNT Sports, TSN, and Sportsnet.

**Peak-hour performance** — Many providers choke during Sunday NFL games or UFC pay-per-view events. The best services use multi-region server architecture with load balancing so your stream never freezes when everyone else is watching the same game.

**4K and HD quality** — Look for H.265/HEVC compression and Adaptive Bitrate Streaming (ABR). ABR automatically adjusts quality if your connection dips, then jumps back to 4K once it stabilizes.

**Device compatibility** — Good services work on Amazon Firestick, Smart TV (Samsung, LG), Android TV, Apple TV, iPhone, Android phones, tablets, Windows, and Mac without extra hardware.

**Internet speed needed** — 10 Mbps for HD, 25 Mbps for 4K. Most US and Canadian broadband connections run well above this threshold.
